About Wild Neighbours

Wild Neighbours helps hikers discover and learn about wildlife on local trails using public wildlife records.

What you can do

  • Browse the animal compendium Search and filter real species records
  • Explore by zone See which animals have been recorded in different areas
  • Log your sightings Keep a personal record of what you've spotted
  • Earn badges Mark your progress as you explore

Where our data comes from

The app uses public wildlife records with a local cache and live ALA updates.

Atlas of Living Australia (ALA)

Species records, names and reference images.

Licence: Varies by record and provider. ALA Terms of Use.

WildNet (Queensland Government)

Queensland wildlife and conservation records.

Licence: Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0).

Wikipedia REST API

Short animal descriptions. Images come from ALA.

Licence: Text is generally CC BY-SA 4.0; check each article.

Some animals have no image because the available data sources do not provide one.

How we treated the data

Our standpoint is to report data accurately and label uncertainty rather than invent missing information.

Rarity is a record-count estimate

Rarity is an app estimate based on cached record counts, not a measure of how common an animal is in nature.

Missing images

We do not show unrelated photos when a species has no available image.

Not a safety guide

Wild Neighbours is for curiosity only. Records may be incomplete or outdated; do not use the app for safety decisions.

Why some animal icons are greyed out

Greyed-out animals are part of the compendium but have not been recorded in the selected zone.

Your data

Progress, sightings and settings are stored in this browser. Photos use IndexedDB. Location is used only for walking and zone matching, and live searches send the selected centre and radius to ALA. There is no app account or backend.

Prototype status

This university prototype may occasionally be slow or unreliable.
